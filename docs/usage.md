# Usage (current state)

All keys except Esc can be changed in `[keys]` of the config, see `config.example.toml`.
Principles and planned bindings (German): `docs/plan-bedienung.md`.

- W: select tool. Click an element on its edge (for unfilled shapes only the edge counts, 6 px tolerance), drag = move, drag handles = resize (corners; for line/arrow the end points; for text the font scales), Del/Backspace = delete, Esc = deselect, double-click on text = edit, double-click on an empty spot = new text. h j k l move the selection (default 10 px, with Shift 1 px, `[move]`), steps in quick succession = one undo step. Color and size apply to the selection. Multi-selection: Shift+click adds or removes, dragging a rubber band on an empty area selects what lies completely inside (Shift: add), Ctrl+A selects everything. Color, size, moving (drag, hjkl) and deleting then apply to all of them, one undo step each; a plain click on one of them selects only that one. Handles only exist for a single element. Ctrl+↑ / Ctrl+↓: bring the selection one step forward/backward, Ctrl+Shift+↑/↓: all the way to the front/back (several keep their order among each other). Q / Shift+Q: rotate the selection 5° counterclockwise/clockwise around its middle (several together around their common middle; blur and markers are left out), presses in quick succession = one undo step; handles keep working on rotated elements. With the mouse: drag the round handle above the frame (single rectangle, ellipse, freehand, text or image; lines and arrows turn via their end points), it follows the mouse freely
- Keys A S D F G T: tools in the order of the config, also by clicking the common bar at the bottom (tools | colors | size, `ui.MainBar`) (default: freehand, line, arrow, rectangle, ellipse, text)
- C: marker. Click = circle with a number, drag = tip where you press, circle where you release (pointer line). C again while the marker is active: switch between 1 2 3 and A B C (counted separately). Deleting renumbers the others, undo restores the old numbers. Color = pen color, size via Alt+A S D F; with W drag tip and circle separately
- Z: blur (screenshot mode only). Drag an area, the screenshot below gets pixelated; block size via the size (Alt+A S D F, Alt+wheel: stroke width × 3). Select, move, resize like a rectangle; whatever lies below is always pixelated (only the screenshot, no markings). When saving (Ctrl+S, Shift+Enter, history) the area is also burned into the embedded raw image: no file contains what was hidden; after reopening, the blur can no longer be undone
- Text: click and type, Esc or a click elsewhere ends the input. Drag existing text = move, double-click = edit
- Labels (text in shapes and on arrows): in the select tool, double-click into a rectangle or ellipse (outline, empty inside or its text) or on a line/arrow and type. On a line/arrow the text sits in the middle of the line, upright, without wrapping (Enter = new line), in a small box (on the whiteboard in its background color, so the line just seems interrupted; on a screenshot in the bar color). For shapes: Esc or a click elsewhere ends the input. The label wraps at the width of the shape (ellipse: a bit narrower), stays centered, has the color of the shape and moves, rotates and resizes with it. It is part of the shape: a click selects the shape, Del deletes both, copying takes it along. Empty the text to remove it. A color picked in the bar while typing recolors shape and label; Alt+A S D F / Alt+wheel change its font size while typing. With the text tool (T), a click into a shape makes a loose text as before
- Connectors (whiteboard only; on a screenshot lines and arrows never dock): a line or arrow whose start or end lands on or near the outline of a rectangle, ellipse, image or loose text (about 16 screen pixels inside or outside) docks there; the shape is highlighted while you drag. A docked end aims at the middle of the shape and stops at its outline, and it follows when the shape is moved, rotated or resized (also with hjkl and undo). Deep inside a shape the end stays free, so you can still point at something inside a frame. Re-dock or undock with the end handle (select tool). Moving or rotating the arrow itself lets go of the shapes that are not moved along; deleting a shape leaves the end free where it was, undo docks it again. Copying shapes together with their arrows keeps the copies connected; a copied arrow alone is free
- Shift+A S D F G Z X C V B: color (order of the color bar), Tab/Shift+Tab cycle
- Alt+A S D F: size in levels 1-4 (stroke width or font size, `[size]` in the config; separate stroke widths for screenshot and whiteboard)
- Alt+wheel: fine size adjustment (text ±2 px, stroke ±1 px per notch), for the selection or the text being typed. Notches in quick succession = one undo step (`PropertyCommand` with `mergeWith`)
- X: crop (screenshot mode only). Drag a frame, then you continue with the tool; darkened outside, the size in pixels at the bottom right. Enter, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+E, Shift+Enter, Ctrl+S and the history output only the crop; the editable file keeps the whole screenshot including the frame, which stays changeable after opening. X again shows handles on the existing crop: drag a handle = resize (corners both directions, edges one), drag inside = move, drag outside = draw a new one. Esc during the selection removes it; everything with undo. With the select tool (W), a click on the edge of the crop selects it: drag its handles to resize and its edge to move it, as often as you like (the inside stays free for the elements in it); a click elsewhere deselects it, Esc removes the crop. Del clears the area whenever no element is selected (the crop does not need to be selected): the screenshot inside is filled with the most frequent color around it (on a web page or terminal: its background), also in saved files, the markings stay, the crop is removed; one undo step brings both back
- Enter: image to the clipboard and quit, Ctrl+C: only copy (with a selection the elements instead)
- Ctrl+C with a selection / Ctrl+V: copy elements and paste them at the mouse, also in another window (e.g. from the screenshot into the whiteboard; xclip holds the clipboard even after quitting). Pasted elements are selected afterwards, markers count on, blur is left out in the whiteboard. Ctrl+D: duplicate the selection slightly offset. One undo step each
- Crop with markings into the whiteboard: mark up the screenshot, X (crop), Enter or Ctrl+C without a selection; Ctrl+V in the whiteboard. The screenshot part arrives as an image element (blur burned in), markers, shapes and text inside stay separate, editable elements. Other programs get the finished image as before
- Ctrl+V with an image from another program (e.g. a browser): pastes it as an image element. Image elements: move, resize at the corners (aspect ratio stays), delete, stacking order; no color/stroke width
- Shift+Enter: save like Ctrl+S (editable PNG, the same file as the next Ctrl+S), absolute path to the clipboard, quit (whiteboard: stays open)
- Ctrl+S: save the editable drawing (PNG with embedded data, a new file in `[output] dir` the first time, after that overwrite the same one), Ctrl+E: export a clean PNG
- `python slate.py image.png`: open a saved drawing again (everything editable) or any PNG as background. Larger than the screen (e.g. from another monitor): shown scaled down, saved in full resolution; smaller: original size, centered
- `python slate.py --last`: open the latest screenshot from the history (editable, changes go into the entry); empty history: a notice, exit code 1. Can be put on an sxhkd hotkey, for example
- `python slate.py --board`: whiteboard in a normal window (background `[board] background`). Esc and Enter do not close it there, Ctrl+Q or closing the window asks if there are unsaved changes. Saved whiteboards (`…_board.png`) open as a whiteboard again automatically. Wheel = pan the view (Shift/tilt wheel/touchpad: sideways), drag with the middle button = pan, Ctrl+wheel = zoom towards the mouse (10–800 %, 7 % per notch), Ctrl+0 = 100 %, Ctrl+W = overview (fit everything, at most 100 %), Ctrl+W again = back to the previous view, as long as you have not zoomed/panned in the overview. Ctrl+B / Ctrl+Shift+B = next/previous background from `[board] backgrounds` (undo step via `Canvas.set_board_color`, saved with the drawing)
- ← / →: history. Every screenshot you change something on is saved automatically (shortly after each change and when quitting; not without changes, `[history]`, by default the last 100 in `~/.local/share/slate/history/`). ← loads the older, → the newer entry into the same window; changes go into that entry again, undo starts fresh there. Screenshot mode only. Opening an entry directly (`python slate.py ~/.local/share/slate/history/….png`) works too; Ctrl+S then creates a separate file in the output folder
- ?: overview of all shortcuts (as currently bound, only what applies in the mode); any key or a click closes it
- b: show/hide the bar (until quitting). Hidden at startup in screenshot mode, visible in the whiteboard (`[ui] show_bar`, `show_bar_board`)
- e: spotlight (everything darkened except a circle around the mouse), Shift+E: magnifier (magnifies around the mouse, 2×). For pointing while teaching; clicks draw nothing meanwhile. Radii, darkening and magnification in `[pointer]`. The same key, Esc or a tool change ends it
- Mouse pointer while drawing: crosshair in the pen color, the fine circle in the middle is as wide as the stroke (including zoom), the shape of the tool at the bottom right. In the select tool an arrow in the pen color with a small dashed selection frame (resize arrows over handles), hidden while pointing
- Ctrl+Q: quit
- R: undo, Shift+R: redo, Esc: quit. All keys except Esc can be changed in `[keys]`, see `config.example.toml`
